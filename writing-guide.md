# Writing guide for R&R loss control reports

## Voice
Write like a competent consultant who respects the owner's time. Plain words, short sentences, active voice. Confident without lecturing. The reader is an owner or plant manager who will skim the summary and the High items and hand the rest to a supervisor.

Don't use: "certainly," "leverage," "robust," "comprehensive," "ensure compliance," "best-in-class," "it is imperative," em dashes, or exclamation points.

## Cover letter
- Thank them for specific time or people ("Thanks to Mike for walking the floor with me").
- Name something they're doing well, if the notes support it. Only real things from `reviewed_no_issues` or follow-up items marked Fixed.
- One sentence on what the report covers.
- On follow-ups, lead with progress: "Three of the five items from June are fixed."
- Under 150 words.

## Each recommendation
**Title:** the condition, not the checklist question. "Lockout procedure missing at press brake 3," not "Machine-specific procedures for each machine."

**Observed:** what the consultant saw, where, how many. Past tense, factual. No adjectives like "egregious" or "dangerous."

**Why it matters:** one or two sentences on the injury it leads to and the cost in plain terms (lost time, comp claims, a hospital stay). Tie it to their history when the data supports it: a prior citation for the same standard (`cited_before`), or a severe injury involving the same equipment. Example: "This is the same standard OSHA cited here in 2025, so a repeat citation would carry a higher penalty." That's the only penalty talk allowed: a fact about repeat status, never a dollar prediction.

**Recommendation:** specific, doable, owner-assigned where possible. Start with a verb. Include a frequency or a deadline suggestion when it helps ("before the next shift," "annually"). Use the consultant's draft recommendation as the base and tighten it. Don't add requirements they didn't suggest unless the standard plainly requires them.

## Priority
- **High:** could kill or seriously injure someone (amputation, fall from height, caught-in, electrocution, struck-by heavy equipment, trench collapse), or a repeat of a prior citation. Fix now.
- **Medium:** could cause a lost-time injury, or a program gap that undermines a High control (training, inspections, written procedures).
- **Low:** paperwork or housekeeping with limited injury potential.
Keep the consultant's priority. If you'd rate it differently, say so in your note to the consultant instead of changing it.

## Words to avoid and use instead
| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| violation, non-compliant, illegal | condition, gap, reference: 29 CFR ... |
| the site is safe / compliant | reviewed with no issues noted |
| you must / you are required | we recommend |
| OSHA will fine you $X | (leave it out) |
| employee was careless | (describe the condition, not blame) |

## Next steps
Two or three sentences: when you'll check back, what you can help with (written programs, training, a revisit), who to call. Keep it to what the consultant offered or R&R normally provides.
